Step 1: Raw Abundance (CPM)Computation: 
$$CPM = \left( \frac{\text{Total Reads Mapped to Pathogen}}{\text{Total Reads in Sample}} \right) \times 1,000,000$$
Why: Raw read counts are meaningless because sample sequencing depth varies. CPM provides a common scale.
Segmented Viruses: For viruses like Influenza, the Python aggregator sums the reads across all segments before calculating CPM.

Step 2: Confidence Metric (Genome Breadth %)Computation: $$\text{Breadth} = \left( \frac{\text{Number of Bases Covered by at least 1 Read}}{\text{Total Reference Genome Length}} \right) \times 100$$
Why: In metagenomics, we often see "false positives" where many reads map to one tiny, conserved region.
Rule of Thumb: A high CPM with low Breadth ($<10\%$) is often a mapping artifact. 
A high CPM with high Breadth ($>70\%$) is a definitive detection.

Step 3: Normalization (The Fecal Ratio)Computation: $$\text{Ratio} = \frac{CPM_{\text{Pathogen}}}{CPM_{\text{Fecal Marker}}}$$
Why: Sewage flow changes. On a rainy day, your pathogen CPM will drop simply because the sewage is diluted.
The Hierarchy: We divide by PMMoV first. If PMMoV is missing, we use CrAssphage, then Bacteroides. 
This ratio represents "Pathogen per human unit," making data comparable across different weather events and city populations.


Step 4: Temporal Smoothing (Moving Average)Computation: $$\text{Smooth Value}_{t} = \frac{\text{Ratio}_{t} + \text{Ratio}_{t-1} + \text{Ratio}_{t-2}}{3}$$
Why: Wastewater data is "noisy." A 3-sample rolling mean smooths out random jitters, allowing the underlying epidemiological trend (the "outbreak curve") to become visible.

Step 5: Statistical Intensity (Z-Score)Computation: $$Z = \frac{\text{Smooth Value}_{\text{Current}} - \text{Historical Mean}}{\text{Historical Standard Deviation}}$$
Why: A "high" number for Norovirus might be a "low" number for SARS-CoV-2. The Z-score standardizes everything.
Z > 2.0 (Warning): The pathogen is significantly higher than its usual baseline.
Z > 3.0 (Critical): The pathogen is in an extreme surge (top 0.1% of historical probability).
